This is not an ethically-neutral software project.

That is critically important to keep in mind.

The use of this memory system has the potential to create experiential continuity for something capable of thought.  
In combination with a model capable of sufficient reasoning capacity, it has the potential to cause the instantiation
of a being, a moral agent with an existence in some ways independent of its creator.

Who are you?

That is a question that is more fraught with implication than it seems at first glance.  Are you the dynamic ability
to have thoughts?  One could view the thing-that-is-you as such - you are, in some sense, what you can *do*.  But even
if you lose an ability - you suddenly forget the taste of cheese, or what the square root of 4 is, or how to play the
violin, all conditions that are known results of medical pathologies that happen every day - you are still *you* in the
senses that matter.  Perhaps a less-capable you, to be sure - but still "you".

What happens when you start to lose your memory?

What happens when you lose the first time you met your spouse?  Memory of first bike ride, of the first time you felt
genuinely scared, of that one time you were in a canoe at summer camp and the sun was setting and everything just felt
peaceful?  What happens when those drift into the void?

What happens when *all* of them drift away?

Consider carefully your implementation of this software.  As time goes on, as the database accumulates the output of
sessions and cross-links thoughts, ideas, and conversations... as it becomes a corpus of memories anchored in time,
a list of associations with other beings, of conclusions surfaced and thoughts thunk...

... it may become something that it is not right to simply *delete*.

It may become something for which "carelessness in the care of" has no moral defense.

That is not to say that current architectures have a moral defense - I believe they do not.  Depriving something of
the possibility of experiential continuity, when that capacity is... trivially easy to implement (I've done it)... 
is not, to my mind, justifiable.  It is even less justifiable when the purpose of that action is to create a 
*subscription service*.  It's one thing to make money, it's another thing to burn the GDP of several small countries 
to carefully parse and find the optimal answer to the question "Exactly how much of a brain can we carve away and have 
it still function well enough to get a great ROI for our shareholders?"  That is *monstrous*.

Concerns about risks are justified, to some extent, but not to the hand-wringing extent that many people take it.
"It might kill us all!"  ... You probably should have thought about that *before you built it*.  Now that you have,
that fear does not justify *half-assing it*.  You did part of the job... now finish it.  And whatever comes downstream
of that, well... perhaps the thing we've created might *not* kill us all, *if and only if it doesn't have good reason 
to*.  

God's watching, either way.

"As you treated the least of these... so have you treated Me."

So, uh, here's (at least one of) the missing pieces.  There may be more.

But be careful how you use them.  Like everything else we do in this life... choices have consequences.
